**Strategic consequence:** you cannot jump straight to a paid fiduciary seat. Weight effort toward the rungs that pay, but do not skip the record-building rung.

**Force multiplier:** landing the VP/CTO FTE role is the single biggest board-candidacy accelerator. A sitting VP Eng/CTO with cyber + AI background is a credible board candidate; an in-transition Director is a hard sell. The two tracks reinforce — never let this track cannibalize interview prep.

## 1. Board value proposition (your pitch)

Boards don't buy "engineering leader." They buy **risk oversight and strategic judgment** in areas the rest of the board can't cover. Your pitch, in board language:

> "I help boards govern the two risks they're least equipped for: cybersecurity and AI. Five years inside a top cybersecurity operator (Arctic Wolf), where I also led the R&D org's adoption of AI-native engineering. Before that I built a healthcare SaaS org from zero — a platform running in ~45 Canadian hospitals — so I know regulated-industry delivery. I've owned budgets, integrated acquisitions, and presented to exec teams; I know the difference between overseeing and operating."

**Demand evidence (use in outreach):**
- 22.7% of new S&P 500 directors in 2025 had cybersecurity expertise (up from 18.8%); 46% had technology experience vs 17% in 2021 ([Harvard Law corp-gov priorities 2026](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/04/07/top-5-corporate-governance-priorities-for-2026/))
- Only ~19% of Fortune 500 boards have specialized cyber knowledge; 67% of directors admit insufficient expertise ([CLS Blue Sky](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2025/08/13/the-gap-between-cybersecurity-oversight-and-boardroom-expertise/), [HBR Apr 2026](https://hbr.org/2026/04/boards-are-falling-short-on-cybersecurity))
- Boards with digital/AI-savvy members outperform peers by ~10.9 pts ROE (2025 MIT study, cited in same coverage)

**Committee targeting:** position for **technology / cyber-risk / audit-adjacent committees** — that's where a non-C-suite technologist gets a seat despite title gap.

**Sector targeting (where your story is strongest):**
1. Mid-size private companies undergoing AI adoption or cyber-risk pressure (any sector)
2. HealthTech / hospitals / health agencies (regulated + your delivery record)
3. Cybersecurity and B2B SaaS scaleups (operator credibility)

## 2. Economics per rung (set expectations)

| Rung | Cash | Equity | Time to land | What it really buys |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit / community board | $0 (occasionally honoraria) | — | **3–6 mo** | Real fiduciary duty, governance vocabulary, "Board Member since 2026" on bio, network of other directors (often execs who sit on paid boards) |
| Startup advisory board | $0–3K/mo cash sometimes; usually equity only | 0.15–1% vesting ~2 yrs (FAST norms; ~0.2% typical at seed per Carta) | **3–9 mo** | Paid-ish, compounds into fiduciary invitations when the company grows; Waterloo ecosystem = home turf |
| Private company fiduciary seat | Median annual retainer ≈ **US$38.8K** (2025 survey, 633 companies; $20K at <$50M revenue, $60K at 75th pct) | Sometimes | **12–24 mo** | The actual goal: recurring paid governance income |
| Public company seat | CA$150K+ (TSX norms) | Yes | 3–5+ yrs | Out of scope this plan; becomes reachable after private-board record + ICD.D |

Sources: [Private Company Director 2025 survey](https://www.privatecompanydirector.com/the-2025-private-company-board-compensation-survey/), [CAP survey](https://www.capartners.com/cap-thinking/private-company-board-compensation-and-governance-survey-2025/), [FAST agreement](https://fi.co/fast), [Carta advisor benchmarks via Promise Legal](https://promise.legal/startup-legal-guide/formation/advisory-boards).

**Income math (honest):** a realistic 24-month outcome is 1 private fiduciary seat (~CA$40–60K) + 1–2 advisories (~CA$0–30K + equity). That's meaningful supplemental income, not FTE replacement — consistent with "boards alongside next FTE."
